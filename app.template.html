How a prediction is made

Every team has an Elo (entry rating depends on the tier of events it plays, tier-1 results count more, shorter stomps count more than long grinds, idle teams regress), and every player has an Elo. The model also uses strength of schedule, average tier played, last-10 form, head-to-head, rest, experience, and how much of the expected lineup played the team's last match. A logistic model fitted on past pro games turns that into a single-game win probability, which is converted to the series (Bo1/Bo3/Bo5) and then blended with the market price using weights fitted on past matches. The roster panel shows who is expected to play, each player's rank among all rated players, who is in or out since the last match, and how many games this exact five has played together. Stakes are quarter-Kelly on the blended probability, capped at 2u; any edge above 8% is treated as suspicious and capped at 0.5u because a model this accurate does not find 8% edges, stale inputs do.
